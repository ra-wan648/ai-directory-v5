What is Video Trimmer?

The Video Trimmer cuts a segment out of your MP4 between two timestamps you enter (mm:ss, e.g. 0:30 to 1:45). A built-in preview player helps you find the right times, and the result downloads as an MP4 clip.

How this video tool works

This tool runs real FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly in your browser. Trimming uses stream copy — the equivalent of ffmpeg -ss start -to end -i input -c copy output.mp4 — so there is no re-encoding: it is fast and the quality is 100% untouched.

Privacy and limits

Your video never leaves your device: the FFmpeg engine downloads (~30 MB, cached after first use) and runs locally. Because trimming is a stream copy, even large files process quickly — but files over ~500 MB may still strain browser memory.

Video Trimmer — frequently asked questions

Is the trim exact to the frame?

Nearly. The fast trim copies the video stream without re-encoding (ffmpeg -c copy), so the cut snaps to the nearest keyframe — usually within a second of your mark. The upside: it is instant and quality is completely untouched.

Which formats can I trim?

MP4 is the primary format and the output is always MP4. MOV and WebM files may also work when their streams are compatible with the MP4 container.

Does my video get uploaded to a server?

No. The FFmpeg engine runs entirely in your browser via WebAssembly. Your video file never leaves your device.

Why is the first trim slow?

The video engine (~30 MB) downloads the first time you press trim. After that it is cached in your browser, and trimming itself takes only seconds since there is no re-encoding.
