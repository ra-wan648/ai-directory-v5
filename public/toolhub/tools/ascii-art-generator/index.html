ASCII Art Generator

Turn any text into ASCII art. Your text is rendered to a canvas and converted from real pixels — cell by cell — into characters. Everything runs locally in your browser.



Tip: paste the result into a code block or anywhere using a monospace font to keep the columns aligned.






How to use ASCII Art Generator


  1. Type your text — Enter a word or short phrase in the input box.
  2. Adjust the detail — Use the character-width slider for more or less detail, and the invert toggle for dark backgrounds.
  3. Copy the art — Hit Copy and paste the ASCII art into READMEs, comments or chat.




What is ASCII Art Generator?
The ASCII Art Generator converts plain text into blocky art made of characters like @, % and #. It is popular for README headers, terminal banners and code comments.


How this writing tool works
Your text is drawn onto a hidden canvas in a bold monospace font. The tool samples the brightness of each cell and maps it to a character from darkest (@) to lightest (space) — genuine image-to-ASCII conversion of rendered text, done with plain JavaScript.


Privacy and limits
Everything happens in your browser; nothing is uploaded. Keep text short for the cleanest result — very long phrases produce very wide output.




ASCII Art Generator — frequently asked questions


How does the ASCII art get generated?
Your text is drawn onto an invisible canvas in a bold monospace font, the real pixel brightness is sampled cell by cell, and each cell is mapped to an ASCII character from darkest (@) to lightest (space). It is genuine image-to-ASCII conversion of rendered text, not a fixed font lookup.
What do the character width and invert options do?
Character width controls the sampling resolution: smaller values sample more cells and give finer detail but wider output. Invert flips the brightness mapping so the art looks right on dark backgrounds or in terminals.
Why does the art look best in a monospace font?
ASCII art depends on every character occupying the same width. When you paste the output somewhere that uses a proportional font, the columns drift out of alignment. Use a code block or any monospace font to keep it sharp.
Is there a length limit?
Very long phrases produce very wide output. The tool caps the rendered canvas so the page stays fast; keep your text to a short word or phrase for the cleanest result.
