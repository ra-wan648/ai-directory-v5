How to use Readability Analyzer

  1. Paste your text — Type or paste the passage you want to score into the text box — analysis updates live as you type.
  2. Read the scores — Check the Flesch Reading Ease gauge, the Flesch-Kincaid grade level and the Gunning Fog index.
  3. Simplify if needed — Shorten long sentences and swap hard words if the grade level is higher than your audience needs.

What is the Readability Analyzer?

This free tool scores your writing with three established formulas. Flesch Reading Ease rates text from 0 (very hard) to 100 (very easy); the Flesch-Kincaid grade converts that to a US school grade level; the Gunning Fog index estimates the years of education needed to understand the text on first reading.

How the scores are calculated

The tool counts words, sentences and syllables with a dictionary-free heuristic that handles silent e, double vowels and common endings. Averages (words per sentence, syllables per word) feed the standard formulas, so results match the classic definitions used by readability checkers everywhere.

What is a good score?

Most online content targets Flesch Reading Ease 60–70 and a grade level of 8–9 — roughly what a 13–15 year old can read. Marketing copy often aims higher (70+). Legal and academic writing scores lower, which is fine if that matches your readers.

Readability Analyzer — frequently asked questions

Is the Readability Analyzer free?

Yes — free to use with no account, no limits and no watermark.

How are syllables counted?

A built-in heuristic counts vowel groups per word and adjusts for silent e and common endings like -ed and -es. It is an approximation, but accurate enough for scoring everyday text.

What grade level should I aim for?

For general web readers, aim for grade 8–9 (Flesch Reading Ease 60–70). Children's content should be grade 5 or below; technical documentation can run higher if the audience is expert.

Does my text leave my browser?

No. All counting and scoring happens locally in JavaScript, so your text never leaves your device.
