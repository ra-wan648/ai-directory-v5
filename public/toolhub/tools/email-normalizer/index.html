How to use

  1. Paste one email address per line into the input box.
  2. Hit Normalize list — addresses are lowercased, trimmed, Gmail dots and +tags removed, and duplicates dropped.
  3. Review the per-line change log, then copy the clean list into your mail tool or spreadsheet.

What normalization does

Every line is trimmed and lowercased. For Gmail addresses (gmail.com, plus googlemail.com which is merged to gmail.com), dots are removed from the local part and everything from the first + is stripped — because Gmail treats first.last+tag@gmail.com as identical to firstlast@gmail.com. Duplicates are detected after normalization, so differently-written variants of the same inbox collapse into one.

Your list stays private

Normalization runs entirely in your browser. Your email list is never uploaded, stored, or sent anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

What Gmail rules are applied?

For gmail.com addresses (and googlemail.com, which is merged to gmail.com): dots are removed from the local part and everything from the first plus sign is stripped, because Gmail treats first.last+tag@gmail.com as identical to firstlast@gmail.com.

How are duplicates detected?

Duplicates are detected after normalization, so First.Last+promo@Gmail.com and firstlast@gmail.com count as the same address. The first occurrence is kept and later ones are listed as duplicates.

What makes an address invalid?

A line is invalid if it has no @ sign, more than one @ sign, an empty local part, or a domain without a dot. Invalid lines are skipped and listed in the change log.

Is this Email Normalizer free?

Yes, it is completely free, runs entirely in your browser, and requires no signup. Your email list never leaves your device.
