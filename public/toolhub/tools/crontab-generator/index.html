    Reading a cron expression

    A cron expression has five fields: minute (0–59), hour (0–23), day of month (1–31), month (1–12), day of week (0–6, Sunday = 0). An asterisk (*) means "every value", a slash (*/15) sets a step, commas list values, and dashes define ranges.

    Day of month vs day of week

    When both day of month and day of week are restricted, cron runs when either matches — not both. For example, 0 9 1 * 1 runs on the 1st of each month and every Monday.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a cron expression?

    A cron expression is a five-field string (minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week) that tells a Unix scheduler when to run a recurring task. For example, 0 9 * * 1-5 runs at 09:00 every weekday.

    Do cron times use my local timezone?

    Yes — the next run times shown here are calculated in your browser's local timezone, which matches how cron behaves on a typical server.

    What does the * character mean in cron?

    The asterisk means "every possible value" for that field. */15 in the minute field means every 15 minutes.
