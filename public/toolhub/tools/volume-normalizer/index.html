How to use

  1. Pick an audio file — choose an MP3, WAV, OGG or M4A file from your device. Nothing is uploaded.
  2. Set the target peak — drag the slider to choose the target peak level (default −1 dBFS). Lower targets leave more headroom against clipping.
  3. Normalize and download — click Normalize volume. The tool scans the true peak, applies one uniform gain, shows before/after waveforms, and your WAV download starts automatically.

What normalization does

The tool decodes your audio to raw samples, finds the single loudest sample across all channels, and multiplies every sample by one fixed gain so that peak lands exactly on your target. The balance between loud and quiet parts is preserved perfectly — the whole track just gets louder or quieter together.

Reading the waveforms

The Before waveform shows your original recording; the After waveform shows the normalized result. If the original already peaked near your target, the two waveforms will look identical — the tool correctly found nothing to change.

Frequently asked questions

Is the volume normalizer free?

Yes. The Volume Normalizer on AI Directory is completely free — no account, no watermark, and no limit on how many files you normalize.

Does my audio get uploaded to a server?

No. Decoding, peak scanning and gain are all done with the Web Audio API in your own browser. Your audio file never leaves your device.

What does −1 dBFS mean?

0 dBFS is the loudest level digital audio can represent — anything above it clips. Targeting −1 dBFS makes the audio as loud as possible while keeping 1 dB of safety headroom against clipping in later encoding.

Does normalization change the sound's character?

No. Peak normalization applies a single uniform gain to the whole track, so the balance between loud and quiet parts is preserved exactly. It only makes everything louder or quieter together.
